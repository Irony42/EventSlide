# Code of Conduct

EventSlide is a small project that people from different countries, backgrounds and
levels of experience may work on together. This page says how we treat each other while
we do. It is short on purpose: the point is to be easy to read and to keep.

It is inspired by the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), a
widely used code of conduct that is worth reading in full. This is a shorter text in our
own words, not a copy of it.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good faith. Most misunderstandings are only that.
- Disagree about the work, never about the person. A code review looks at the change, not
  at the author.
- Be patient with newcomers, with people writing in a second language, and with questions
  that have been asked before.
- Give credit, take feedback without sulking, and say so when you are wrong.
- Keep the project's users in mind: guests at an event, the hosts who run it, and the room
  looking at the screen. They are real people.

## What we do not accept

- Harassment, insults, threats, or demeaning comments, in any form or place the project
  runs.
- Discrimination or attacks based on who someone is, including their age, disability,
  ethnicity, gender or gender identity, nationality, appearance, religion, sexual
  orientation or experience.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or imagery.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as an address or a private message,
  without their consent.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion, and anything else a reasonable person would call
  unprofessional in a workplace.

## Where this applies

In this repository: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and commit messages. It
also applies when someone speaks for the project elsewhere, and to a conflict outside it
that makes the project unsafe for another participant.

## Enforcement

The maintainer, Pierre Tijou, is responsible for applying this page. He may edit or remove
comments, close or lock a thread, decline a contribution, or block an account, for as long
as he thinks fit, and he will say why when it is safe to do so.

To report a problem, write to **[contact address to be filled in by the maintainer]**.
Reports are read by the maintainer only, are kept private, and are answered as fast as one
person reasonably can. If a report is about the maintainer himself there is nobody else to
take it yet, and GitHub's own tools for reporting abuse are the place to go.

Reports about a security vulnerability are a different thing: follow
[docs/SECURITY.md §13](docs/SECURITY.md#13-reporting-a-vulnerability).

## Changes

This page can change. The history of the file in the repository is the record of how.
